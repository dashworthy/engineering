# Force-calibration checklist — force matched to how fragile the task is

The lens for the force-calibration check. Imperative force — "YOU MUST", "Never", "Always", "No
exceptions" — removes the agent's discretion. It is right on a narrow bridge with cliffs, wrong in an
open field where many paths reach the goal. The rule: reserve force for low-freedom, safety-critical,
and discipline-enforcing steps; let guidance and reference skills stay light. Both directions of
miscalibration are findings. Contents:

- Force inflation (too much)
- Under-forced cliff (too little)
- Calibrating the fix

## Force inflation — too much force in an open field

"YOU MUST" / "Never" / "Always" on more than the one or two rules that would cause real damage if
skipped. It backfires twice: it is wrong on its face (there isn't one mandatory path), which teaches
the agent the skill exaggerates and discounts its genuine warnings; and force does not stack — if
every line shouts, nothing stands out and the two rules that truly must not break drown in the noise.
This is banner blindness.

Tells:

- Imperative force on a step with several valid approaches.
- Force on nearly every heading or bullet, so none reads as the exception.
- Emphatic wording ("critical", "absolutely", "under no circumstances") on a routine preference.

**Fix:** demote all but the genuine cliffs to plain guidance. Keep the imperative on the one or two
rules that would cause real damage if skipped; phrase the rest as the plain guidance it is.

## Under-forced cliff — too little force on a narrow bridge

The rarer, more dangerous direction: a genuinely fragile step — a migration that must back up first, a
destructive operation, a safety gate, a discipline that only works if never skipped — written as a
gentle suggestion a rushed agent will rationalize past.

Tells:

- A destructive or irreversible step ("this deletes", "this overwrites", "this is not reversible")
  worded as "you may want to" or buried in plain prose.
- A discipline whose whole value is that it holds under pressure (test-first, verify-before-claiming)
  stated without the force that closes the rationalization.

**Fix:** raise the force to match the cliff — a clear imperative, and where it earns one, a one-line
statement of what breaks if the step is skipped.

## Calibrating the fix

Before proposing either direction, ask the degrees-of-freedom question: **is this a cliff, or just
the busiest path across an open field?** Only the cliff earns the imperative. Match each step to its
own terrain rather than the whole skill to one level — a high-freedom skill can still have one
low-freedom step where the cliffs are, and that one step keeps its force.
